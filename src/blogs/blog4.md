## We're Afraid of Silence

Pull out your phone at a red light. Put on a podcast while cooking. Fall asleep to YouTube. We fill every gap with noise and call it productivity.

But what are we actually avoiding?

Pascal wrote that all of humanity's problems stem from the inability to sit quietly in a room alone. That was the 1600s. He hadn't seen a smartphone.

## Solitude Is Not Loneliness

This distinction matters. Loneliness is wanting connection and not having it. Solitude is choosing to be with yourself — and actually being okay there.

Most people never learn the difference because they never sit long enough to find out.

## What Happens When You Stop Running

When you strip away the noise, the first thing you notice is discomfort. Thoughts you've been outrunning catch up. That's not a bad thing. That's the whole point.

Seneca said we suffer more in imagination than in reality. Most of the anxiety we carry exists because we never sit with it long enough to see how small it actually is.

## The Mind Needs Unstructured Time

Every insight I've had about my own work, my own direction, what I actually want — none of it came during a productive sprint. It came on a slow walk. In the shower. Staring out a window with no agenda.

The mind needs room to wander before it can find anything worth keeping.

## It's a Practice

You don't become comfortable with solitude overnight. Start small. Eat one meal without your phone. Take a walk without headphones. Sit with your morning coffee and just drink it.

It feels strange at first. Then it starts to feel like the most honest part of your day.
